## 0.6.7 (unreleased)

### Added

- Personal pinyin dictionaries: import or update UTF-8 Rime `.dict.yaml` vocabulary in Settings → Input → Chinese (pinyin), see each file's name and entry count, and remove it. Dictionaries supplement rime-ice, redeploy automatically, and stay on this Mac (words picked from them are learned, and sync with the learned words when that's on). Source files, existing Rime customizations, learned words and fuzzy-pinyin settings are preserved; unsupported files are refused before changes.
- English words in pinyin learn from English mode too: a word you type twice in English mode (after a tap of Shift) comes up among pinyin's candidates from then on, with nothing to do. English mode itself is as it was: the keys go straight to the app and nothing is shown there. A word is the letters between a space or a mark and a space, ⏎, a mark or a tap of Shift back to Chinese; one you edit with ⌫ (while typing it or right after), one with a digit or a mark inside (example.com, don't, abc123), or one with a capital first (a name) isn't learned. The same guards and the same second time as letters put in as typed while typing pinyin: never in password fields, terminals and SSH clients, remote desktops and virtual machines, password managers (a password shown in a plain field there), or apps with No AI; never pinyin, a word rime-ice has, anything that looks like a key or a number or holds one of your hidden words; the first time only remembered in memory. Privacy: every word typed in English mode is now looked at, on this Mac only, and nothing is sent anywhere for it; a word learned is kept in `englishWords` in your config file, and leaves this Mac only when Sync through iCloud is on with the learned words. The switch "Learn the English words you type" turns it off with the rest. Windows doesn't learn from English mode yet.
- Autocomplete: pause while you write and the rest of the sentence shows in gray after the caret; Tab puts it in (only while it shows: ⇧Tab, ⌥Tab and a Tab within 120 ms of it appearing go to the app). Typing along with it shrinks it with no new request; Esc or any other key takes it down (Esc means "not this one": it doesn't come back for that text in that app); ⌘Z right after Tab takes it back, counted as not taken. One switch, Autocomplete, in the input menu and the menu bar panel (also in a sentence in Settings → Assistant, in the interface's languages: "turn on autocomplete", "我要把 auto complete 关闭"; one that only turns it on or off is done as it says without asking a model), off by default. It asks the AI provider picked in Settings → AI Provider, the one the @ commands use, with its model and settings (Bedrock, Claude API, Gemini, OpenAI-compatible); nothing asks first and nothing is kept: the switch says where the text goes, on or off ("Autocomplete (Claude API · claude-haiku-5-5)", and "Each pause sends up to 1,500 characters before the caret (and 200 after) to …, hidden words removed", sensitive information hidden as for the @ commands), and so do Settings → Assistant's row and its confirmation. A small, fast request: 48 tokens back, no thinking where the model can be asked for none (Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5 and 5.5, Opus 5), the lowest effort otherwise (a model that always thinks, or a reasoning model, may answer too late to show). Where the provider can't answer (no key, the network, a refusal) nothing shows, no other model is asked, and it says so once per text field. With Apple Intelligence picked, the model on this Mac (loaded ahead as a text field takes the focus; where it isn't available nothing shows and nothing is read). Never AllInOneIME Cloud, whose free requests a day are kept for @ commands: with it picked, the model on this Mac, and turning it on says "Uses Apple Intelligence; set your own AI provider for faster suggestions". After a pause that follows how you type (half as long again as your usual time between keys, at least 350 ms on this Mac, 500 ms with a cloud provider), only once the line has a word to go on from, only at the end of a line, never while pinyin or a command is being written, in password fields (those Accessibility tells too), terminals, password managers, remote desktops, apps with No AI or while Translate as You Type is on; code editors (Xcode, VS Code, Cursor, JetBrains…) are off unless set to always. About a dozen words or 25 Chinese characters at most, to the end of the sentence; the last 20 kept, each for its text before and after the caret in its app. In an app where none of the last 30 suggestions was taken it stops by itself and says so once; Settings → Apps sets it to always or never per app, each Mac for itself (not synced). The gray text stops at the field's edge where Accessibility tells it. What it shows and what you take is counted on this Mac only. In the config: `autocomplete`, and `autocomplete` in an app's profile.
- Suggestions: plugins can complete what you type, as rows in the candidate panel, and Tab puts one in. `@github` (in the plugin library) lists your repositories' issues and PRs after `#`: `#12` shows `#1234 Fix login…`, Tab adds `34`; in chat apps it adds the issue's address too. A plugin keeps its list on this Mac (refreshed while you use it, every 15 minutes for `@github`) and may also look up what you type after its prefix, from 2 characters on (`@github`: numbers only). `@github <words>` searches the titles in the repositories you set, with your token. Up to 5 rows, the ones you picked before first (by how often and how recently); only what you haven't typed goes in, as you typed the rest. In English and in pinyin (`#` while pinyin is composed puts the words in first). Keys by app: Tab, ↓ ↑ then ⏎, and Esc in most apps; only Tab in chat apps and browsers (⏎ always sends there); until ⏎ would take it, the first row is only outlined, not selected (Tab takes it); Tab completes what the rows share in terminals and code editors; none in launchers, remote desktops and virtual machines; digits never pick a row. Rows take the place of autocomplete's gray text. Writing one: `completions` and `preferences` in plugin.json, `load(ctx)` and `query(text, ctx)` in its script ([docs/plugins.md](docs/plugins.md)). In the config: `completionSources`, `pluginPreferences`, `suggestions`, and `suggestions` in an app's profile. Windows keeps the settings but shows no rows yet.

One whose pack isn't installed downloads it first, from the language library and checked against its signed list, as adding an input language downloads its pack (its progress, Cancel and any failure with Retry on the page); the packs installed (each removable: deleting the pack of Code's language removes Code) and the library are on the Input page. - The magic key, the symbol commands start with, can be changed in Settings → General from `@` to another symbol: `#`, `%`, `&`, `*`, `+`, `|` or `~` (picked from a menu; symbols that typing needs aren't in it). Command suggestions, nested commands, and custom or plugin commands use the chosen symbol; `@` becomes ordinary text when another symbol is selected. Existing settings keep `@`. The text that names commands follows it too: the panel and notices, Settings, the input menu, the menu bar panel and the installer. It syncs through iCloud with the other settings (a symbol this version refuses never comes in).

### Changed

- Voice input is turned off for now; your setting is kept.

### Fixed

- Candidates appear and disappear without window animations, including notices and the Translate as You Type chip. Committing a word hides the panel immediately; continuous typing reuses its window, which closes after 400 ms of idle time. This avoids repeated window transitions while typing in apps such as Slack and Notes. An unchanged panel frame no longer triggers another resize or shadow update.
- The input menu reuses the cached config instead of parsing `config.json` for every request. A broken file still shows an error and disables choices that would overwrite it; repairing the file refreshes the menu.
- Pinyin's lunar date (`nl` and `NYYYYMMDD`) finds its bundled database in a fresh Rime user folder, copied again when a language pack or app update brings a new one. An existing database of your own is kept, and a copy that can't be made only leaves the lunar dates out: pinyin starts all the same.
- Settings → Assistant → Check for problems names the error librime logged while the lunar date's database was missing ("Rime: a file it needs is missing: lua/lunar.db (the lunar date)") instead of "error in Rime" three times, and lists each event in the logs once, with how many times it came up in the last week. Once the file is in place (this version puts it there), those lines show as an earlier error that is resolved, not as a warning.
- Check for problems' "Accessibility isn't allowed" now says what to do when System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility already shows AllInOneIME on: after an update macOS can keep the permission for the previous version, so turn it off and on again there (Fix opens that pane). The check is the running input method's own permission.
- Models that refuse the temperature no longer fail: reasoning models, and Claude models on Bedrock that won't take it along with top_p ("`temperature` and `top_p` cannot both be specified for this model"). On Amazon Bedrock, the Claude API and OpenAI-compatible services, such a request is sent again once without it, and the model is remembered (in `~/Library/Caches/AllInOneIME/unsupported-temperature.json`, never `config.json`), so its next requests leave it out from the start; Settings → AI Provider → Model Settings says how many are remembered, and Reset, by the temperature switch, sends it to them again. The same for Settings → Assistant and Check for problems: on Bedrock they ask with a temperature of 0; with the Claude API and OpenAI-compatible services they send the one set for the provider, if any (as every request there does). Other models still get the temperature you set.
- Settings → Assistant says "Already set that way" only when what is already so is what you asked for. An answer that turns a switch the other way than your sentence says ("turn on typing out" answered with it off, as it is), or only keeps settings your sentence isn't about as they are, now says what wasn't understood: "Couldn't tell whether to turn Type results out on or off", "Couldn't tell which setting that is about".

## 0.6.6 (2026-10-20)

### Added

- English words in pinyin: type a few letters and the English words they start come up among the candidates (bedr → Bedrock; bedrock → Bedrock first). Besides rime-ice's own: yours, the English terms of your jargon list, and about 220 product and technical words rime-ice lacks (Bedrock, SageMaker, DynamoDB, LangGraph…). The same letters put in as typed twice while typing pinyin (bedrock ⏎, or a tap of Shift) are learned, with no command (the first time they're only remembered, until the app quits): never in password fields, terminals and SSH clients, remote desktops and virtual machines, or apps with No AI, never pinyin (rime-ice's corrected spellings and your fuzzy pinyin too), nothing that looks like a key or a number or holds one of your hidden words. Settings → Input → Chinese (pinyin) → English Words lists yours, to add (what looks like a key, a card or phone number or an IP address is refused; one it learned becomes yours) and remove, with the switch "Learn the English words you type". They sync through iCloud with the learned words, merged by their letters; past 2,000 the oldest learned go first, never one you added. A Mac updated from 0.6.5 takes the words and the switch your other Macs have, never puts its defaults over them. Nothing is deployed again for a word added. In the config: `englishWords`, `addedEnglishWords`, `learnsEnglishWords`.

### Fixed

- Slack and other apps built on Electron or Chromium: holding Shift turns Translate as You Type on and off there too, a tap of Shift switches the input language once (it could switch twice when the page was busy), and a tap of ⌥ runs the action key. These apps hand each press of a modifier key to the input method twice; the copy is now told apart (tested with an Electron app built like Slack's).
- Settings → Assistant on Apple Intelligence: no more "context too long" error. The request is fitted to the on-device model's context (only the settings the sentence is about when the whole doesn't fit); when even that doesn't fit, your own AI provider answers instead and the page says so before sending, or the page says what to do. When you ask for a provider by name ("use Bedrock") and the on-device model answers with another, that change isn't offered. An AWS account number in a Bedrock model or profile name is not sent to other providers.
- Apple Intelligence's "too long" message in every interface language.
- `@translate` with nothing to translate into but the sentence's own language (English alone, as on a new Mac with no other language): Settings no longer opens by itself. Opened from the key, the window either took the focus at once, which hid the notice and put `@translate …` into your text as typed, or, where macOS kept the focus in your text, came up without it, with nothing on the notice saying so. Now the notice stays and names the page: "No language to translate into yet: ⏎ opens Settings → Translate & Rewrite to add one". ⏎ (or Space) on it puts the sentence in as typed and opens that page with its Add… outlined; a language picked there works at once. While no language other than the ones you type is added, the page says so under Languages.
- Translate as You Type with only the languages you type to translate into (English alone): it no longer turns on and then does nothing, but says there's no language to translate into, and where to add one. With one added after English (French), it translates into that one at once, not into English: until you pick a language on its chip, the language you type is passed over when it's the only one. The same when the language it translates into is removed in Settings while it's on: the first one left that what you type can be translated into, else it turns off and says where to add one (it stayed on into English, doing nothing).

## 0.6.5 (2026-10-18)

### Added

- `@reply` writes three replies to the message you're answering, in their language, in the tone of the app: `@reply politely decline`. Give it the message by selecting it, copying it, or taking a screenshot (⌃⇧⌘4, or WeChat's own) and pressing ⌃V in the draft. The screenshot is read on this Mac and only its text is sent. ⌘R gives three more. A screenshot pasted into any other command works too: `@translate` ⌃V translates it. Your own `@reply`, if you had one, is now `@myreply`.
- Snippets: a custom command can now insert text you saved, such as a signature, an address or a meeting link: `@sig` ⏎. Placeholders: `{input}`, `{name?}` blanks filled from what you type (separated by commas), `{clipboard}`, `{selection}`, `{date}`, `{time}` (with `{date:FORMAT}`), and `{cursor}` for where the caret ends up. Write `@sig` inside another command's text to include it. Create them in Settings → Custom Commands → Insert text. Extension sources can ship them (`snippets` in `extension.json`). A custom command of a type this version doesn't know is now kept in the config file as it is, and its name stays taken. **Update every Mac that syncs through iCloud:** 0.6.4 can't read the custom commands once one is a snippet, and if you then add, edit or delete a custom command on a 0.6.4 Mac, its list replaces the one in iCloud and the snippets are gone from your other Macs too.
- API keys and passwords can sync through iCloud Keychain, end-to-end encrypted: Settings → General → Sync through iCloud → "API keys and passwords" (off by default, `iCloudSync.keys`: turning on sync alone moves no key). Turning it on moves this Mac's keys from the login keychain into iCloud Keychain, so macOS no longer asks for the login password for them after updates; turning it off copies them back into the login keychain and leaves them in iCloud Keychain for your other Macs. Keys that don't sync stay in the login keychain, as before. When two Macs have different keys, the one saved last wins; the other is kept in its Mac's login keychain as a replaced copy that is never used ("AllInOneIME replaced API key", named "… (replaced)"; one per key, a later one replacing it), and Remove… on that Mac deletes it. Removing a key while syncing removes it on every Mac that syncs keys; a Mac with key sync off, or one still on 0.6.4, keeps its own copy and puts it back if it turns key sync on later (the question says so). On a Mac with key sync off, Remove… removes it only on that Mac. A key that needed your login password when it was to move stays in the login keychain, and can come back if you allow it when macOS asks. The AllInOneIME Cloud sign-in stays in each Mac's login keychain. While keys sync, older versions and `allinoneime-cli` don't see them in the keychain; shell variables still work.
- Translate as You Type (a prototype to try): hold Shift on its own for 1 s to turn it on or off (a hint at 0.5 s; a
  tap of Shift still switches the input language), with a notice near the caret for about 1.5 s naming the language it
  translates into ("Translate as You Type on: Chinese → English · hold Shift again to turn off"); while it's on, the
  menu bar icon carries a small dot. What you type collects into a draft, and ⏎ translates it and inserts the
  translation at once, with no candidates and without sending it: into the language picked last on its chip, in every
  app and after a restart (until you pick one, or once that one is no longer a translation language: your first
  translation language; the language you type has no say). The chip, "ZH → EN" (「中 → 英」 in the Chinese interface),
  shows near the caret while it's on, in the candidate panel (it never takes the focus): click "ZH" to switch the
  input language as a tap of Shift does, "EN" for a list of your translation languages to pick another at once. ⇧⏎
  inserts the draft as typed, Esc while it's translating keeps the draft (⌫ deletes a character from it), and a
  failure keeps it with a notice (⏎ tries again). The tone follows the app: its first rewrite style, else casual in
  chat apps, formal in mail apps, natural elsewhere. There is no command for it. Switching apps (to one with no text
  field too) turns it off, with no notice, unless the app has "Always Translate as You Type" in Settings → Apps
  (`translateAlways`). In an app with No AI or during secure input, text goes in as typed. Its request is one of its
  own: every other prompt is unchanged.
- Script plugins get the input method's language: the app calls `run(input, {language})`, `language` being `"zh"` or `"en"` as its own interface shows (config `uiLanguage`, else the system's). Plugins with `run(input)` work as before; nothing else is passed.
- `@agent` in Google Chrome takes the page along, so you can ask about what's in front of you: `@agent why is this blocked` on a pipeline page, `@agent why doesn't this work` in a sign-in form. After what you typed, the agent gets the address and title, the field you typed in (its label, whether it's marked invalid) with its form or section, ★ marking where you are, the selected text, and what's visible in the window; with the focus in the address bar, the section under the mouse pointer. Never a password field, never the whole page: the agent decides whether the question is about that spot or the page, and can open the address in your browser for more. It needs Accessibility; Chrome builds its page tree for it only while `@agent` is being written there (its accessibility is turned back off after). The notice says "with the page “…”". Settings → Agent → "@agent and @reply take the Chrome page along" (`agentPageContext`, on). `AllInOneIME --page-context` prints what it would take along from the Chrome page in front.
- Sync through iCloud: Settings → General → "Sync through iCloud" (off by default) keeps your settings, jargon list, custom commands and the words pinyin learned the same on all your Macs, through your own iCloud Drive (`AllInOneIME/` there): no other sign-in, nothing through our servers. Each part has its own switch: Settings, Jargon and custom commands, Learned words (Rime's user dictionary, through librime's own sync) on; Hidden words (iCloud Drive is end-to-end encrypted only with Advanced Data Protection) and Recent questions off. Each setting carries when it changed and on which Mac, and the newest change wins setting by setting (per-app settings app by app; removing one is a change too); a Mac turning sync on takes the settings in iCloud already and merges its collections with iCloud's (recent questions, hidden words, custom commands by name, jargon lines), and one turned back on after a while off takes what iCloud changed meanwhile and sends only what it changed itself; a Clear of the recent questions clears them on every Mac. It syncs when the input method starts, about 5 s after a change, when iCloud brings one from another Mac and on Sync Now (the learned words every 30 minutes, after a pause in typing); the status line says "Last synced 2 min ago", "Waiting for iCloud…" or the error. A value this Mac can't use (a provider without a key here, a value out of range) isn't applied, and Settings → Assistant's Check for problems says which; a file iCloud hasn't downloaded yet is waited for, never taken as empty; an unreadable `settings.json` is set aside, never overwritten; settings of a newer version are kept. Never synced: keys and passwords, extension sources, extension folders and plugins, window state, the sync switches. Without iCloud Drive the switch says to turn it on in System Settings → Apple Account → iCloud, with a button there. "Remove from iCloud…" (asked first) moves the folder to the Trash. Settings → Assistant can turn sync on or off (the opt-ins only in Settings → General), and its Check for problems checks it. In the config: `iCloudSync`.
- The interface in seven more languages: 繁體中文 (Traditional Chinese), 日本語, 한국어, Deutsch, Français, Español and Português (Brasil). It follows the system's preferred languages (a region in its language: de-AT is Deutsch; zh-TW and zh-HK are Traditional Chinese; Portugal gets Brazilian Portuguese), or the language picked in Settings → General, where each is listed in its own language.
- @note can save elsewhere: Settings → Plugins → Notes (@note) → Save to: Notes (as before), Notion (an integration token kept in the keychain, and the link of a page or database; Test checks both), or a folder of Markdown files (an Obsidian vault or any folder; a file per note, named after its first line). The notice says where the note went, and the menu bar panel opens it there. During secure input, @note doesn't send to Notion. In the config: `notes`.

### Changed

- A recorded action key is now also saved by its place on the keyboard, so it is the same key on any keyboard or system; older versions still read it.
- The interface is ready for more languages (its text goes through a String Catalog). Its language is the one picked in Settings → General, else it follows the system's preferred languages (System Settings → General → Language & Region): the first of them the app has.
- Change settings in a sentence and Check for problems moved to Settings → Assistant, the page Settings now opens on (it no longer comes back to the page shown last). Write what you want ("translate into Japanese and English", "candidates in a column") and press Preview (or ⌘⏎): each change shows old → new, Apply writes them and Undo puts them back within 10 minutes. The checks run as the page appears; Check runs them again with Test Connection, and with something in "What's wrong? (optional)" the AI explains the likely cause. A row that can be fixed has a Fix button: settings changes go through the preview above, the rest act at once. The same checks, rules and privacy as before. The input menu's "Add Languages…" items open the page with the languages.

### Removed

- `@config` and `@doctor`: use Settings → Assistant. Typed, they are now unknown commands, and a custom command, a plugin or a script extension may take the names.
- The built-in `@calculate` is removed: the calculator is now the `@calculator` plugin in the plugin library (Settings → Plugins → Browse Library…). It works offline, with the same rules as before. Inside a sentence, write the expression without spaces or quote it: `@reply the total is @calculator 23*17 dollars`, `@calculator「2 * pi」`.

### Fixed

- Web pages read for the AI no longer keep runs of blank space: spaces, tabs and no-break spaces in a row inside a line become one space.
- Stricter checks of the script an extension bundle (`extension.json`'s `"script"`) or a plugin (`plugin.json`'s `"script"`) names: a path that starts with a drive letter (`C:x`) or a backslash (`\x`, `\\server\x`) is refused, like one that starts with `/` or climbs out with `..`: a script must be a file inside its bundle's folder as written. An extension source's bundle path that climbs out of the repository with `..` loads nothing, as before (other paths, `/x` too, are folders of the repository), and now so does one with a NUL character in it. A language pack with a Windows program or script in it (`.exe`, `.dll`, `.bat`, `.ps1`, …) is refused as code, like one with a `.sh`. Extensions, plugins and packs that worked before still do.
- An extension bundle's commands keep their other paths inside the bundle too, from the script's folder as written: an `icon` image, a `status` program given as a path (`./left.sh`) and a `currentDirectoryPath` that isn't `~/…` or absolute. One that leads out (`../icon.png`, `/usr/bin/pmset`) is a bad value: the command doesn't load, and Settings says why. A `status` program named alone (`date`) is still looked for in the script's folder, then on the PATH. The review before Enable now also shows what each command's status line runs. Your own single scripts and Raycast scripts are unchanged.
- Sync through iCloud no longer loses a change while a Mac is turning sync on, or back on: another Mac's newer edit, a setting you save in Settings meanwhile (or right after), and custom commands or hidden words added on either Mac are all kept. A custom command or hidden word deleted on one Mac stays deleted on the others, also after more edits on top. (In three cases a deleted one comes back once, and deleting it again sticks: it came from the first Mac to turn sync on, and that Mac was asleep while another Mac merged its list in; it was deleted on a Mac still on 0.6.4; or a Mac still on 0.6.4 brings it back.)
- Settings: saving a custom command whose name another Mac took meanwhile keeps the editor open and says the name is taken, instead of closing with your command lost. Editing the hidden words no longer brings back a word another Mac deleted while the field showed it. Of two custom commands with the same name (a config edited by hand), Delete and Edit act only on the one you clicked.

## 0.6.4 (2026-10-16)

### Added

- Amazon Bedrock with every common kind of AWS profile, refreshed by itself, in the AWS CLI's order within a profile (a role, SSO, the credentials file's keys, `credential_process`, the config file's keys): static keys as before, `credential_process` (`credential_process = /usr/local/bin/my-creds --profile dev`: split like a shell command and run without a shell, with your login shell's PATH, in your home folder, 60 s at most), a role (`role_arn` with `source_profile`, chains too, a profile naming itself with its own keys, or `credential_source = Environment`; `role_session_name`, `external_id`, `duration_seconds`; STS in the profile's region), SSO (`sso_session` or the older `sso_start_url`, with the sign-in the AWS CLI cached, only read), and for the profile "default" (or none) the keys in your shell's environment first, as the AWS CLI takes them (never for a profile you name); `AWS_CONFIG_FILE` and `AWS_SHARED_CREDENTIALS_FILE` are followed. Credentials that expire are kept in memory only and fetched again 5 minutes before they do, in the background when a request comes with less than 10 minutes left; requests at the same time wait for one fetch; Bedrock answering that they expired gets them fetched again and the request sent again, once. Settings → AI Provider lists each profile with its kind ("dev — Role via base") and says of the chosen one when its credentials expire ("Role via base: credentials expire at 15:30", "SSO (signed in until 18:00)"); Test Connection fetches fresh ones through the whole chain; `@doctor` reports the kind, the expiry and a failed refresh. An SSO sign-in that ended says "SSO sign-in expired: run aws sso login --profile X" (Copy Command and Open in Terminal in Settings, the command to copy in `@doctor`); a profile with `mfa_serial` says to use `credential_process` or SSO instead, since an input method can't ask for an MFA code; `credential_source = Ec2InstanceMetadata` or `EcsContainer` says why it can't work on a Mac. Programs run and AWS is asked only when a request or Test Connection needs credentials, never to list profiles or for `@config`; nothing secret is logged or shown.
- `@config`: change settings by describing them (`@config output languages English and French`, `@config 微信里用中文，邮件里语气正式一点`, `@config candidates in a column`, `@config use Claude`). ⏎ shows the changes in the candidate panel, a row each, old → new, with the labels Settings uses; ⏎ (or 1) applies them, Esc cancels, typing more and ⇧⏎ refines them, and ⌘Z on the confirmation or `@config undo` within 10 minutes puts back exactly what changed. Only an allowlist can change: the languages, rewrite styles, candidate layout, typing out, the selection, recent questions, hiding sensitive information (words only added), updates, per-app settings (apps found by name) and the provider's non-secret settings (provider, Bedrock profile and region, a known model, an `https://` base URL); keys, custom commands, extensions, plugins and anything that runs programs are refused with the reason (a value that looks like a key is never shown, nor the model's note with one), and every change is checked as Settings checks it (a language pack that isn't installed points to Settings). A provider without a key (none in the keychain or the shell) isn't switched to: its row says the key is needed and applying opens Settings at its key field (choose it there once the key is saved); with a key it switches and Test Connection runs. A key typed into the sentence is never sent. It runs on Apple Intelligence whenever this Mac has it (held to the allowed settings, with lookups for your apps, AWS profiles and language packs), else on your provider; the request holds only the allowed settings' current values.
- `@doctor`: checks the setup on this Mac and fixes what it safely can. ⏎ lists the results, ✗ problems first, then ⚠ and ✓, a row each with a short fix: the input method, the AI provider (key saved or Bedrock credentials found, never read; Test Connection; Apple Intelligence), permissions (asked of macOS without a prompt), secure input, language packs, `config.json`, extensions and plugins, updates, and recent errors in the app's logs. A digit previews that row's fix in `@config`'s preview and ⏎ applies it; ⏎ on the list fixes the safe ones (settings repaired, the input method enabled again); settings changes undo like `@config`'s, and nothing deletes your data or touches a key. In an app with no AI the checks still run and only the question is refused. `@doctor <question>` then has the AI explain the likely cause and suggest fixes, on Apple Intelligence when available (the checks are its tools), sending only the check results (paths as ~, keys removed) and the question; ⏎ still applies only the safe fixes.
- `@question` conversations: once an answer is in, type the next question and press ⇧⏎. The panel grows into the conversation, earlier rounds dimmed as Q / A rows and the next question's line on top with its round ("question 2/5 › …"); it grows to about half the screen, then scrolls. Every round's answer can be inserted (its digit 1–5, ↑↓ then ⏎, a click; the latest is selected) and ⌘C copies the selected one; Esc clears what you typed, then goes back one round. Each follow-up is sent as a normal conversation (the questions and answers so far, then the new question); the first question is sent exactly as before. With Apple Intelligence on this Mac a long conversation is fitted into its small context (older answers shortened, then the oldest rounds left out) and the panel says "earlier rounds shortened". Up to 5 rounds (`questionRounds` in the config, 2–10).
- Continue an `@question` conversation in Claude or another AI: after the last round the next question's line becomes "5 rounds in: continue in Claude or another AI", and ⌘⏎ shows the same choices at any round. ⌘1, ⌘2 … pick a coding agent installed on this Mac (the one `@agent` hands to first, then Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Kiro CLI or your own command, each an interactive session in Terminal started as `@agent` starts it; one that isn't installed isn't shown), Claude or ChatGPT on the web (the conversation filled in, or on the clipboard when it's long), Gemini on the web (on the clipboard), or copying the conversation as Markdown; what you type then is the first message there (only ⌘1–⌘9 send it; ⇧⏎ there opens nothing). A question about selected text takes that text along as a quoted block of its own. Which ones and in which order: Settings → General → "Continue a conversation in" (`questionContinueIn`); its "@agent's agent" (`agent`) follows the agent picked for `@agent`, and an agent not installed right now keeps its place when you edit the list. The conversation goes, as shown, only to the service you pick.
- Recent questions keep a whole conversation under its first question, and picking it shows its rounds to go on with; files from 0.6.3 load as before.
- Extension sources: install a script-extension bundle from a Git repository and keep it up to date from there. Settings → Custom Commands → Extension Sources → "Add from Link…" takes an `ssh://`, `git@host:`, `https://` or `file://` address (optionally a branch, the bundle's folder in the repository, a tag or commit to pin to), clones it shallow with your own git and SSH setup (keys, agent, ~/.ssh/config, your own ssh command; no terminal and no prompts, 60 s a call) into a staging folder under `~/Library/Application Support/AllInOneIME/Sources/`, and shows what the bundle holds before anything loads: its commands with what they output and the programs they run, its preferences, the commit, under "This source isn't signed: it will run programs from <address>". Only Enable puts it in place and adds it to the extensions; Cancel deletes the staged clone. Only reviewed code loads: a source folder in a copied config isn't loaded until it's reviewed here. Sources are checked once a day (and about 30 s after the input method starts) unless turned off (`autoCheck`) or pinned: "N updates" with the new commits and a badge on the page, and nothing changes until you click Update, which fast-forwards with hooks off (a force push needs the review again: Re-clone shows it). Errors show git's own words, a hint to try `git ls-remote` in Terminal, and your own `authHint`. Remove moves the clone to the Trash and keeps the bundle's preferences. From a terminal: `--add-source`, `--update-sources`, `--list-extensions`. In the config: `extensionSources` (`url`, `path`, `branch`, `pin`, `autoCheck`, `authHint`). An added folder that is itself a bundle (has an `extension.json`) now loads as that bundle.

## 0.6.3 (2026-10-15)

### Fixed

- Opening Settings no longer asks the keychain ("AllInOneIME wants to use your confidential information…") for the keys of services you don't use: whether a key is saved is told without reading it, a key is read only when a request needs it, and once allowed it isn't asked for again until the input method restarts or the key changes.
- Settings → Custom Commands no longer fills with orange "the name is taken" and "Another bundle with this name is loaded" warnings when several copies of a folder are on the Mac (git worktrees, a copied repository): from a folder Spotlight found, only its Raycast Script Commands are loaded now (not its `@allinoneime` scripts or bundles; add the folder to use all of it), byte-identical copies of a script or bundle are skipped with one grey line ("Skipped N identical copies"), and found folders since removed drop out of the list.

### Changed

- AllInOneIME is signed with a Developer ID (MicroZ LLC) and notarized by Apple: the installer opens like any app downloaded from the internet (macOS asks once whether to open it), with no more allowing it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. The disk image is notarized and stapled, and the apps in it and in the in-app update are signed with the same Developer ID.
- Pinyin and Hindi candidates are side by side by default now (Horizontal), like the system's own input methods: ← → choose, ↑↓ (or - =) turn the page. This also applies after updating, unless you choose otherwise: for the column as before, choose Vertical under Settings → Input → "Candidate layout" (`"candidateLayout": "vertical"` in the config).
- In the @ command list, a command whose name is exactly what you typed comes first: `@rep` lists your own `@rep` before `@reportissue` and `@reply`, however often those are used.

### Added

- Refine the results: while the results of `@improve`, `@translate`, `@question`, a prompt command or the rewrite of a selection are up, type a follow-up instruction (`shorter`, `warmer`, `more formal`, `add a joke`, `in Japanese`, `用更口语的说法`; pinyin works, Shift switches languages) and press ⏎: the AI gets the original request, the results shown and your instruction, and the new results replace them in the same panel, in the same rows. Refine again and again; the panel's top line lists the steps ("Refined: shorter · more formal"), and a row can be picked at any time as usual. A letter starts the instruction ("refine › …" at the top of the panel) once the results are complete (typing while the first results stream in continues the sentence, as before); with none typed, digits, Space and ⏎ pick as before. While a refine runs, the next instruction can already be typed: the refine goes on, and ⏎ runs the next one once its results are in. Esc clears the instruction, then goes back one step to the results before the last refine (during a refine, it stops it), then to the sentence; ⌫ on an empty line steps back the same way. On selected text the pick still replaces exactly the selection, untouched until then. A refine that fails, or is refused during secure input, leaves the results as they were with the instruction ready to try again; the same refine of the same results comes from the cache. The requests for everything else are unchanged.
- Commands on the selected text: select text in any app, type `@improve`, `@translate`, `@question` (or another command that takes text) and ⏎, and the command works on the selection; the result you pick replaces exactly it. The selection stays as it is, still selected, while you type: the command is written in the candidate panel instead of the text field, its first line what you type (pinyin too) and the next what it works on ("On the selection: “…” (N characters)"). Esc, ⇧⏎, clicking elsewhere or secure input turning on leave the text as it was, with no `@improve` typed over it; changing the selection meanwhile cancels the command. Text typed after `@improve` / `@translate` is an instruction (`@translate ja`, `@improve more formal`), answered as one rewrite that keeps the text's paragraphs (as is a selection of several lines); after `@question` it's the question about the selection. `@open`, `@claude`, `@tasks`, `@settings`, `@reminder`, `@imessage` and script extensions don't use it. Read through the text input system, or with the Accessibility permission where an app doesn't tell it (only if AllInOneIME already has it; it never asks); up to 5000 characters; never during secure input or in a terminal; only how it was read and its length are logged. On by default: Settings → General → "Use the selected text" (`useSelection` in the config).
- Updates from inside the app: Settings → About → Check for Updates, and "Check for Updates…" in the input menu. When a new version is out, About says so with what's new (and one quiet line in the menu bar panel; never a dialog); "Update to <version>" downloads it, checks it, installs it and restarts the input method, keeping your settings, language packs, learned words and extensions. AllInOneIME looks once a day by itself, in the background, and never goes online because of a key you press ("Check for updates automatically" under About, `checkForUpdates` in the config, on by default); nothing is installed until you click.
  - The update information (https://allinoneime.com/update.json) is signed with a key of its own, for releases only, and checked with the public key built into the app before it's read; the download comes only over https from allinoneime.com or the GitHub release, and is installed only once its size and SHA-256 match, both apps' bundle IDs, versions and code signatures check out, and the new input method starts. An older or the same version is never installed.
  - The old apps are moved aside, not deleted, until the new ones work: if anything goes wrong on the way, the version you had is put back and registered again, and About says what happened.

A field that ignores where to replace text keeps that first typo (the text one character off, also after Esc or another key), and its app gets no typos again, until "Forget apps where typos couldn't be fixed" under the switch.
- Per-app settings: Settings → Apps gives an app its own starting input language (Slack in English, WeChat in pinyin), its own rewrite styles for `@improve` (Mail formal, Slack casual), typing results out on or off, and "No AI in this app", where `@improve`, `@translate`, `@question`, `@claude` and prompt commands send nothing and say so, while typing and the other commands work. Each is optional and follows the setting for every app when unset. "Add App…" lists the apps typed in lately or picks one in /Applications; the input menu has "This App: <name>" with No AI, Type Results Out and the starting language. In the config: `appProfiles`, by bundle ID; `@reportissue` counts them, never naming the apps.
- Click a row of the candidate panel to pick it, as its digit would: a pinyin or Hindi word, a command in the @ list, a result, a file `@open` found (as you type too), a recipient, a row an extension listed. The row under the pointer is highlighted, and the text field keeps the focus. (thanks [@jiwenwumc](https://github.com/jiwenwumc))
- `@question` lists your recent questions: with nothing typed after it, the candidate panel shows the last 5 you asked, newest first, and typing narrows them down (words anywhere in the question, any case, Chinese too). A digit, ↑↓ then ⏎, or a click shows a question's saved answer at once, without going online, to insert (⏎, Space) or copy (⌘C) as any answer; R there asks it again for a fresh answer, which takes the old one's place. With no match, or nothing picked, ⏎ asks what you typed, as before. Settings → General → "Remember recent questions" (`rememberQuestions` in the config, on by default) turns it off and deletes them; "Clear" deletes them.
- Apple Intelligence on this Mac as an AI provider (macOS 26 and later): choose "Apple Intelligence (on this Mac)" under Settings → AI Provider and `@improve`, `@translate` and `@question` run on the model built into macOS, with no key, no cost, and nothing leaving the Mac (`"provider": "apple"` in the config). It gets the same prompts as the other providers, with only the examples in the sentence's own language. On a Mac where it can't be used it is greyed out with the reason (not supported, turned off, model not ready) and a button to System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri; before macOS 26 it isn't listed. Test Connection works with it, and `@reportissue` reports the provider as "apple".
- Hide sensitive information from the AI (Settings → AI Provider → Privacy, off by default): before any request goes out, with every provider including AllInOneIME Cloud, email addresses, phone numbers, payment card numbers (Luhn-checked), API keys and tokens (`sk-`, `sk-ant-`, `AKIA…`, `ghp_`, `xox…`, long random strings), IP addresses and words you list (a codename, a name) are replaced on the Mac with placeholders such as `[EMAIL_1]`; the results get the real values back before they're shown or inserted, and the panel says "2 items hidden from the AI". A result whose placeholder the model changed beyond recognition is marked "⚠ placeholder changed". Each kind has its own switch; names and addresses aren't recognized (list them as your own words). Applies to requests to your AI service (prompt commands and prompt plugins included); `@claude`, plugins that fetch from a website (`@stock`) or open one (`@x`) and script extensions send text as typed. `@reportissue` says only whether it's on. In the config: `redaction`.

### Added

- Script extensions: @ commands that are programs outside the app, in any language, with a few lines of metadata in their comments (docs/extensions.md, protocol 1).   - What they print can be inserted (`insert`), shown as it comes with nothing inserted (`show`, a progress), offered as a list to pick from as you type (`list`, Alfred's Script Filter JSON: picking a row inserts, opens, copies or runs a second step), or said near the cursor (`silent`); a JSON `summary` with a `detail` shows both.

  - Scripts get `ALLINONEIME_*` environment variables: the interface and input language, the app you're typing in, the text.

  - Raycast Script Commands work as they are, and folders holding them are found with Spotlight (on by default; hide the ones you don't want).
  - Settings lists the folders, the bundles, every command with a switch, and the scripts left out with why (a protocol newer than the app's among them). Examples of each kind are in Examples/extensions.

  - Dictation (hold right ⌥) listens in the language you're typing.
  - `@improve` keeps a Korean, Hindi or French sentence in its own language, and `@translate` translates it into each of your languages but its own (English and Chinese both, with those two). (French is told apart from English when French is one of your input languages.)
- Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi and Urdu as translation languages. Hindi typed in Latin letters ("aap kaise ho") is translated into Devanagari, each in its own script; a Mac set to one of them (hi-IN …) translates into it from the start.
- ← → move the cursor in a command's text (`@question …`), as in any text field: type, pinyin, ⌫, a paste or speech go in where it is. ⌘← ⌘→ (or Home, End) go to the start or end of the text, ⌥← ⌥→ a word at a time, fn⌫ deletes after the cursor.
